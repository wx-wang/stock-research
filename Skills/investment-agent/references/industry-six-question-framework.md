# Industry Six-Question Coverage Contract v1.4

This reference adapts the investor-approved six-question industry-reading method to the existing Investment Agent workflow. It is an analytical coverage contract, not a new workflow, scoring model, gate, or Skill.

Use it for sector discovery rough cuts, full industry research, and the industry/profit-pool stages of named-company research. Preserve the investor-mandated 12-step industry process and 9-stage stock process.

## 1. Industry Scope Card

Before answering the six questions, define the research object:

```text
Industry / Subsegment
Final Product or Service Boundary
Substitute Boundary
Technology Path
Geographic and Market Boundary
Value-Chain Position
Lifecycle Context
Competition-Structure Context
As-of Date
Units and Statistical Definitions
```

Use at least two relevant classification views when the object is composite: product/service, technology path, lifecycle, geography plus value-chain position, and competition structure. This classification defines the research object inside the Granularity Gate; it is not an opportunity-classification framework.

If material businesses, demand structures, technology paths, or profit pools remain mixed, return `Recheck—Subsegment` and decompose the scope before an industry-wide conclusion.

## 2. Six Questions

### Q1 — Size, Growth, and Cycle Position

Answer how large the industry is, how fast it is changing, and where it sits in time.

Minimum coverage when material:

- market size with geography, period, units, inclusion/exclusion rules, and source;
- growth versus a named historical baseline, preferably across a cycle rather than one favorable year;
- penetration, participant count/concentration, margin direction, and capital-expenditure intensity when relevant;
- nested cycle position:
  - lifecycle: Introduction / Growth / Maturity / Decline;
  - development phase: Technology Breakthrough / Capital Investment / Commercial Validation / Profit Release / Mature Competition;
  - shorter cycle: inventory, capacity, industry-price, or macro sensitivity as applicable;
- expected transition, approximate verification horizon, and evidence that would falsify the cycle call.

Do not treat lifecycle, development phase, and inventory/capacity cycle as interchangeable.

### Q2 — Industry Map and Profit Distribution

Answer how value moves through the industry and where profit is concentrated or migrating.

The `Industry Map` should contain:

```text
Node
Node Type: Main Chain / Lateral Support / Platform / Critical Technology
Transaction or Dependency Relationship
Representative Participants
Revenue / Cost / Margin Direction
Pricing Power and Scarcity
Current Profit Concentration
Expected Profit Migration
Evidence Level and Unknowns
```

Start with the main chain, then add lateral-support, platform, and critical-technology nodes only when they materially change the economics. Distinguish:

- supply chain: physical and operational flow;
- enterprise value chain: activities inside one company;
- industry chain/map: relationships and profit distribution across companies.

Technical sophistication alone is not proof of a profit pool. Test patents, network effects, brand/customer lock-in, scale/learning, regulatory or resource scarcity, and the competitive response.

### Q3 — Segment Economics, Moats, and Competitive Advantage

Answer which mechanisms allow a segment to retain profit and what winner characteristics are required.

Minimum coverage when material:

- Five Forces plus relevant complementors, network/data effects, government/regulation, and M&A-driven structural change;
- business model and its key economics;
- potential moat mechanism: scale, switching costs, network effects, brand/intangibles, regulatory/resource privilege, data effects, or ecosystem lock-in;
- durability, profitability, and measurable evidence;
- segment ROIC or a fit-for-purpose return metric versus capital cost, history, and peers, with accounting/cycle caveats;
- supply replication, price-war, substitution, and entrant response.

At industry level, Q3 produces `Winner Characteristics` and a segment-level advantage hypothesis. It must not conclude that a named company has Company Alpha. That conclusion belongs to `investment-company-alpha` and requires company-versus-competitor evidence.

### Q4 — Technology Evolution and Disruption Risk

Answer how technology may change the chain, cost curve, profit pool, or existing advantages.

Minimum coverage when material:

- current mainstream technology and its S-curve position;
- credible next-generation routes and representative participants;
- performance, cost, yield, qualification, commercialization, and adoption milestones;
- sustaining versus disruptive mechanisms where the distinction is useful;
- impact on incumbent assets, bottlenecks, value per unit, profit pools, and moat durability;
- separate judgments for direction, winning route, and commercialization timing.

Do not convert a laboratory result, product roadmap, or management claim directly into commercial adoption.

### Q5 — Regulation, Geopolitics, and Localization

Answer how non-market forces reshape access, pricing, supply, technology paths, and profit distribution.

Minimum coverage when material:

- licensing/access, government pricing or procurement, antitrust, environmental/carbon, data/security, exceptional intervention, and industrial policy;
- tariffs, export controls, sanctions, local-content rules, and critical geographic dependencies;
- cross-market regulatory differences where comparables span A-share, Hong Kong, or overseas markets;
- localization by subsegment rather than one industry-wide label;
- quantity share, value share, technology-node capability, top-customer adoption, international share, and technical/patent evidence where available;
- technology-driven versus policy-forced substitution and the durability of the path if policy support normalizes.

Localization success is not automatically investment return. Test competition, price decline, reinvestment needs, customer quality, international expansion, and whether profit remains after substitution.

### Q6 — Industry Valuation Adapter and Narrative-to-Numbers

Answer how the first five questions translate into market expectations and an industry-appropriate valuation frame.

Minimum coverage when material:

- valuation methods appropriate to business model, lifecycle, cyclicality, and geography;
- current valuation context versus history and relevant peers, with consistent definitions;
- current market narrative and its evidence level;
- narrative-to-numbers bridge: growth, duration, margin, capital intensity, return on capital, cash flow, and/or terminal assumptions;
- 3P narrative test: Possible / Plausible / Probable;
- at least Bull / Base / Bear industry scenarios when uncertainty is decision-critical;
- what the market may be under- or over-pricing and the next observable test.

At industry level, Q6 identifies the valuation adapter, dominant narrative, and preliminary priced-in assumptions. It must not issue a named company's fair value, upside/downside, or portfolio state. Those belong to `investment-earnings-valuation` after Company Alpha is validated.

## 3. Dynamic Entry Order

Run a rough scan of all six questions before choosing the deep-dive order. Use the industry's dominant driver as a routing aid, not a rigid classification:

| Dominant driver | Typical first deep-dive |
|---|---|
| Resource / commodity | Q1 |
| Technology | Q4 |
| Regulation / policy | Q5 |
| Structurally stable competition | Q2 then Q3 |

All material questions must still be covered before the final industry decision. Record `Dominant Driver`, `Recommended Deep-Dive Order`, and the reason for the order.

## 4. Integration with the Existing 12-Step Process

| Existing step | Six-question use |
|---|---|
| Granularity Gate | Industry Scope Card and multi-view boundary check |
| Investment Opportunity Hypothesis | Six-question rough cut, dominant driver, critical Unknowns |
| Industry Thesis | Primarily Q1, Q4, and Q5; integrate all material constraints |
| Industry Cycle | Q1 nested-cycle record |
| Value Chain & Profit Pool | Q2 Industry Map and profit migration |
| Competitive Dynamics | Q3 segment economics and winner characteristics |
| Market View vs Agent View | Q6 narrative-to-numbers and preliminary priced-in assumptions |
| Catalyst / Verification | Verification events and falsifiers from all six questions |
| Beneficiary Candidates | Map validated profit-pool nodes to candidates for Company Alpha research |

The Scanner performs a rough cut and routing decision. The Industry Value Chain Analyzer performs the deep read. Do not duplicate the full deep read in both Skills.

## 5. Six-Question Coverage Record

Use one compact record rather than six new gates:

| Q | Status | Core conclusion | Decisive evidence/change | Constraint on other questions | Confidence | Unknown / next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Allowed status values:

- `Supported`: sufficient for the current industry-stage conclusion;
- `Recheck`: missing, conflicting, stale, or not yet observable evidence could change the conclusion;
- `Not Material`: genuinely immaterial for the defined scope, with a short reason.

Do not mark a question `Supported` merely because it was discussed. Coverage status is not bullishness and does not replace Pass / Recheck / Reject.

## 6. Cross-Question Constraint Loop

After the first pass, explicitly test interactions. At minimum consider:

- Q4 technology → Q3 moat durability and Q2 profit migration;
- Q5 regulation/geopolitics → Q2 profit distribution and Q4 route choice;
- Q1 cycle/lifecycle → Q3 return metrics and Q6 valuation method;
- Q3 supply response/business model → whether Q1 growth becomes profit;
- Q6 priced-in assumptions → which Q1–Q5 assumption is most aggressive;
- localization in Q5 → whether profit is retained under Q2/Q3 rather than competed away.

Record only decision-relevant interactions in `Cross-Question Constraint Map`.

## 7. Gate Effects

Do not create six additional Pass/Recheck/Reject gates. Preserve Granularity, Industry Thesis, and Profit Pool gates.

- A critical Q1–Q5 `Recheck` that could reverse value creation, profit-pool location, or durability prevents an overall industry Pass.
- A Q6 `Recheck` prevents a strong variant/pricing conclusion when market expectations cannot be identified, but it does not by itself falsify an otherwise valid long-term industry thesis.
- `Not Material` is acceptable only with a scope-specific reason.
- Industry Pass still means “eligible for company research,” not “buy.”

## 8. Monitoring Baseline

For every material question preserve:

```text
Original Conclusion
Key Variable and Definition
Baseline Value / State
Expected Direction
Verification Event or Window
Falsification Condition
Source and Date
```

Monitoring should update only changed questions, identify the earliest affected Skill, and preserve the original baseline. Redraw the Industry Map when a material node, dependency, technology route, regulation, or profit-pool relationship changes; do not impose an arbitrary calendar rewrite when nothing material changed.

## 9. Boundaries

- Do not add a new Skill, opportunity classification, automatic score, or six independent gates.
- Do not hard-code illustrative penetration, ROIC, valuation, or time thresholds as universal rules.
- Do not confuse industry-level winner characteristics with Company Alpha.
- Do not confuse an industry valuation adapter with a company valuation conclusion.
- Do not use a complete framework as evidence that the answers are correct; preserve Unknowns and conflicting sources.
