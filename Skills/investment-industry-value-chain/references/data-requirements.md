# Data Requirements

## Upstream Context

Required:

- candidate industry or named company's industry;
- dated trigger fact and preliminary change hypothesis;
- market state and risk context;
- as-of date;
- current market narrative;
- unresolved inputs from the scanner.
- preliminary Industry Scope Card, Six-Question Rough Cut, dominant driver, and recommended deep-dive order when available.

## Industry Scope and Definition Data

- final product/service and material substitute boundary;
- technology path and material alternative routes;
- geographic and market boundary;
- value-chain position and subsegment definition;
- as-of date, units, inclusion/exclusion rules, and statistical definitions;
- at least two relevant classification views for composite objects.

## Industry Thesis Data

- demand size and growth with geography, period, units, inclusion/exclusion rules, and baseline;
- users, applications, penetration, participant count/concentration, margin direction, and capital-expenditure intensity when material;
- demand drivers and comparison baseline;
- supply, capacity, inventory, resources, and entry conditions;
- technology, cost, product, policy, or consumption changes;
- lifecycle evidence;
- expected duration and current catalyst;
- key substitute or competing solution when relevant.
- lifecycle position, development phase, and applicable inventory/capacity/price-cycle evidence kept distinct;
- expected phase transition, verification horizon, and falsifier.

## Value-Chain and Competition Data

- main-chain, lateral-support, platform, and critical-technology nodes when material;
- products, services, and transaction relationships by segment;
- volume, price, cost, and margin direction by segment;
- capacity, expansion plans, and cost curve where material;
- market shares and their changes;
- leading participants, new entrants, and possible exits;
- evidence of scarcity, pricing power, customer power, scale, brand, or network effects;
- current and expected profit-pool location.
- segment business model and key unit economics;
- fit-for-purpose return evidence such as segment/company-peer ROIC, ROE, asset turns, or cash return, with capital-cost, accounting, and cycle caveats;
- relevant complementors, network/data effects, regulatory force, and M&A-driven structure changes;
- winner characteristics without assuming a named company has Alpha.

## Technology Evolution Data

- current mainstream technology and S-curve position;
- credible next routes and representative participants;
- performance, cost, yield, qualification, commercialization, and adoption milestones;
- sustaining/disruptive mechanism where useful;
- impact on value-chain nodes, bottlenecks, capital assets, profit pools, and moat durability;
- separate confidence for direction, route winner, and commercialization timing.

## Regulation, Geopolitics, and Localization Data

- licensing/access, pricing/procurement, antitrust, environmental/carbon, data/security, exceptional intervention, and industrial policy as material;
- tariffs, export controls, sanctions, local-content rules, and critical geographic dependencies;
- cross-market regulatory differences for relevant comparables;
- localization by subsegment using quantity share, value share, technology-node capability, top-customer adoption, international share, and technical/patent evidence where available;
- technology-driven versus policy-forced substitution and profitability after competition/reinvestment.

## Industry Valuation and Narrative Data

- valuation method suited to business model, lifecycle, cyclicality, and geography;
- current industry/segment valuation versus history and relevant peers on consistent definitions;
- current market narrative with identifiable source and evidence level;
- narrative-to-numbers assumptions for growth, duration, margin, capital intensity, return, cash flow, and/or terminal conditions;
- Possible / Plausible / Probable test and Bull/Base/Bear industry scenarios when decision-critical;
- preliminary priced-in assumption and next observable validation.

## Minimum Pass Data

Industry Thesis requires evidence for demand or value creation, supply response, lifecycle, duration, and why now.

Profit Pool requires evidence for segment economics, pricing power, competitive response, and earnings direction.

The Six-Question Industry Card must mark each material question `Supported`, `Recheck`, or `Not Material` with a reason, confidence, critical Unknown, and next check. A critical Q1-Q5 Recheck that could reverse the industry or profit-pool conclusion prevents an overall Pass. Q6 evidence may remain Recheck only if the report avoids a strong variant/pricing conclusion and clearly preserves the gap.

If the industry data is valid but segment economics are missing, return Industry Thesis Pass and Profit Pool Recheck rather than a combined positive conclusion.
